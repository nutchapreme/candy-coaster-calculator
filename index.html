Operating constraints and interpretation

Dispatch estimate: The calculator takes the largest of fleet cycle time ÷ number of trains, station occupancy, and the user-entered block clearance interval. It is an illustrative scheduling lower bound, not a verified safe dispatch interval.

Blocks: N trains need at least N + 1 independently controllable blocks (the station counts as one), so one block is always empty between trains.

Track length: No universal minimum length guarantees two-train operation. Separate controllable blocks with proven stopping and restart capability are required. Braking distances, vehicle geometry, and clearance must be validated physically.

These are simplified theoretical models. v₀ is the train's speed at the top of the drop (0 = released from rest). Retained energy is applied to the drop only. Lateral g uses the energy-adjusted bottom speed as a conservative case; turns higher on the layout will be gentler. Average speed is an independent input; measure it during testing. Uptime and retained energy are assumptions, not predictions.
